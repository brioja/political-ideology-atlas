# Political Ideology Atlas

A political ideology test where the axes come from the data. You don't choose them in advance.

## How it works

1. Every ideology in `audits/catalog.json` has a sourced audit giving its answer (-2..+2) to every
   statement in `statements.json`. `node scripts/build.js` compiles the valid audits into
   `data.js`.
2. `pca.js` centers the ideologies × statements matrix and eigendecomposes its covariance (Householder
   + QL, no dependencies). Each eigenvector is a dividing line; its eigenvalue is how much of the
   disagreement between ideologies that line captures. The build keeps the lines that stand out
   from noise (`meaningfulAxes`, compared against shuffled data).
3. The leading lines (at most 6, `SHOWN` in `scripts/build.js`) are varimax-rotated so each one is
   a single cluster of statements, then named from `audits/line-labels.json`. A name holds only
   while its anchor statements stay among that end's strongest; otherwise the build warns and the
   page names the ends by ideologies until the labels file is updated.
4. Ideologies are ranked by the average gap between your answers and theirs. The results show the
   top matches, flag close calls, and list the statements where you agree and disagree with your
   top match.

Adding a statement or an ideology means adding its audit data and rebuilding. The lines and how
many are kept update on their own.

Ideologies get the same salience check as people (see below), counting only their **stated**
positions (high or medium confidence). An ideology whose answers are mostly inferred is left out of
both the results and the dividing lines.

## Sharing results

The results page has a share link (`?r=2.` followed by one character per active statement) that reopens
the same results for anyone, and a downloadable 1200×630 image.
Nothing is stored on a server. `og.png` is the preview image for links to the home page; it is
generated by `drawCard` with no result.

Changing the active statements breaks older links. While the question bank is still growing that
is accepted; stable links are a release concern.

## Ideology audits

`audits/IDEOLOGY_STANDARD.md` says what counts as an ideology. Each one in `audits/catalog.json` gets a sourced audit at `audits/profiles/<id>.json`:
its answer to every statement with a rationale, confidence and citations, plus a Wikipedia link
and further reading. `audits/PROMPT.md` holds the exact research instructions, so any audit can
be re-run.

- `node scripts/pending.js` lists ideologies whose audit is missing or invalid (`--backfill`: audits
  that still need answers for new statements).
- `node scripts/validate.js [id ...] [--links]` checks audits (add `--links` to test every URL, `--no-salience` to skip the slow salience check).
- `node scripts/build.js` compiles valid audits into `data.js` (`--status` just reports progress).
- `node scripts/wikipedia.js` refreshes suggested Wikipedia links in the catalog.

To run every pending audit, ask Claude Code to "run the ideology-audits workflow"
(`.claude/workflows/ideology-audits.js`). It costs roughly 75k tokens per ideology and is safe to
stop and resume, since finished audits are skipped. Pass `{"limit": 10}` to do a batch.

## Statements

`audits/statements/STANDARD.md` says what makes a statement worth asking (a real, sourced
position that ideologies actually disagree on) and how new ones are added. In short: audits
propose them (`missingStatements`), they join `statements.json` as candidates, the
statement-backfill workflow answers them in existing audits, and `node scripts/statements-check.js`
shows when one is contested enough to be asked. Adding or removing a statement leaves existing
audits valid; rewording one needs a new id.

## People

`audits/people-catalog.json` lists about 2,800 public figures taken from data, not hand-picked:
people Wikidata lists with a political occupation (including political theorists, philosophers,
economists and cause activists) or a political office, ranked by English Wikipedia views
(`scripts/candidates.js`). Nobody is added or dropped by hand; to change who is in, change the
criteria in that script. `node scripts/people-report.js` reports the mix of gender, region and
era, and an imbalance is reported rather than corrected by hand. Each gets
an audit at `audits/people/<id>.json` following `audits/PROMPT_PEOPLE.md`: positions come from the
person's own words and record, and a statement with no reliable evidence is `null` instead of a guess.

- **Salience:** a person is included only if their recorded positions place them on the test's
  real dividing lines more precisely than the typical (median) distance between an ideology and
  its nearest neighbour there (Bayesian placement uncertainty, see `salienceContext` in `pca.js`).
  A less precise record can't say which of two neighbouring ideologies it is closer to.
  It depends on which statements have a recorded position, not on the answers, and has no
  hand-picked cut-off. Negative controls (`audits/controls/`, apolitical celebrities audited with
  the same instructions) must fail it; `node test.js` checks this. Excluded audits make the
  validator exit with code 2 so they aren't re-audited.
- **Tie-break:** when two people are within 3 points of each other, the one with more English
  Wikipedia page views (last 12 months, from `node scripts/pageviews.js`) ranks first. The counts
  are stored in the catalog; the site never calls Wikipedia.
- **Photos:** `node scripts/images.js` stores each person's freely licensed Wikipedia lead image
  (URL, author, license) in the catalog; the site shows it with a credit line and falls back to
  initials when there's no free image. It only looks up people without an image; run it with
  `--refresh` when tagging a release to pick up newer lead images and credits.
- To run the audits, ask Claude Code to "run the people-audits workflow" (about 75k tokens per person).

## How it was made

Built with the assistance of AI. The audits are researched by AI agents following the published
instructions in `audits/`, and every answer cites the sources it used, so any of them can be
checked and challenged.

## Run locally

Open `index.html` in a browser, or run `python3 -m http.server` and visit http://localhost:8000.

`node test.js` checks the eigensolver and verifies that every ideology matches itself. It also
prints the axes it found.

## Deploying

It is a static site with no build step and no server, so any static host works:

- **GitHub Pages:** push this folder to a repo, then turn on Settings → Pages → deploy from branch.
- **Netlify / Cloudflare Pages:** drag the folder in, or connect the repo. There is no build
  command and the publish directory is `/`.

## Data flows one way: ideologies → people

The person audits are AI-researched estimates of public figures' views, not their own answers, so
they are not ground truth for anything else. Ideology data may shape how people are shown
(placement, matches, comparisons), but nothing about the ideologies, statements, dividing lines,
salience, labels, colors or quiz calibration is derived from people data. This keeps the
subsystems loosely coupled: a change to the people catalog or a person audit never moves the test.
